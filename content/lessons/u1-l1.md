title: What data science is

A streaming service suggests your next show. A hospital flags patients likely to be readmitted. A weather service gives a 70 percent chance of rain. Each is data science at work: data turned into a useful answer. The test opens its largest area with the basics of the field, so you need crisp, exact answers to four questions: what data science is, what it is made of, who does it, and what kinds of questions it answers.

## Data science in one sentence

**Data science** is the field that uses statistics, computing, and knowledge of a subject area to turn data into insight and decisions. Each part matters:

- **Math and statistics** let you reason about patterns and uncertainty: is this difference real or just noise?
- **Computing** lets you collect, clean, and analyze data at a scale no person could handle by hand.
- **Domain expertise**, knowledge of the subject the data describes, lets you ask the right question and judge whether an answer makes sense.

In 2010 Drew Conway drew a Venn diagram that made this idea famous. Its three circles are **hacking skills** (computing), **math and statistics knowledge**, and **substantive expertise** (domain knowledge). Data science sits where all three overlap.

```figure
type: venn
caption: Conway's view of data science. Each pair of circles alone falls short; data science needs all three.
sets:
  - { label: Hacking skills }
  - { label: Math and statistics }
  - { label: Substantive expertise }
center: Data science
```

The pairwise overlaps are useful test material. Hacking skills plus math and statistics, without domain knowledge, Conway labeled **machine learning**. Hacking skills plus domain knowledge, without statistics, is the diagram's **danger zone**: someone who can produce analyses that look convincing but may not be valid.

Here is how the three combine in practice. A hospital wants to predict which patients will return within 30 days. Statistics builds and tests the model. Computing pulls years of records out of databases and cleans them. Doctors and nurses supply the domain knowledge: which factors matter, and what counts as a readmission in the first place.

:::key
**Key idea.** Data science is interdisciplinary: statistics plus computing plus domain knowledge, aimed at turning data into insight and decisions.
:::

## Data, information, knowledge, and data literacy

The words "data" and "information" are often used loosely, but data science uses them precisely. The **DIKW pyramid** orders four levels from bottom to top:

1. **Data**: raw facts with no context. 101.8.
2. **Information**: data given context and meaning. 101.8 is this patient's temperature in degrees Fahrenheit at 9 a.m.
3. **Knowledge**: patterns and understanding built from information. A temperature above 100.4 means fever, and this patient has had one for three days.
4. **Wisdom**: judgment about what to do. Order tests and begin treatment.

```figure
type: pyramid
caption: The DIKW pyramid. Each level is built from the one below it.
levels: [Wisdom, Knowledge, Information, Data]
```

The pyramid is usually credited to Russell Ackoff's 1989 article "From Data to Wisdom", whose version also included a level called understanding between knowledge and wisdom; most modern versions leave it out.

**Data literacy** is the ability to read, work with, analyze, and communicate with data, including the ability to question it. A data-literate person asks where numbers came from, whether a chart's conclusion follows from its data, and what is missing. Organizations that make **data-driven decisions** base their choices on evidence like this rather than on hunches alone.

:::trap
**Common trap.** Information is not just "more data." It is data with context attached. A bare number is data until you know what it measures.
:::

## Who does the work [Competitive]

Data science is a team sport, and the test expects you to match tasks to roles.

| Role | Main question | Typical work |
|---|---|---|
| Data analyst | What does the data say? | Queries existing data, builds reports and dashboards, explains findings |
| Data scientist | What will happen, and why? | Frames open questions, builds predictive models, designs experiments |
| Data engineer | Is the data available and reliable? | Builds and maintains pipelines, warehouses, and data infrastructure |
| Machine learning engineer | Does the model work in production? | Deploys models into products, scales them, monitors their performance |

A few other roles appear in questions:

- A **database administrator** (DBA) keeps databases secure, backed up, and running fast.
- A **data steward** is responsible for a set of data: what each field means, who may use it, and tracking its quality. This is part of **data governance**, which you will meet in lesson 1.6.
- A **business intelligence** (BI) developer builds the dashboards and reports a company uses to track its performance.

:::tip
**Test tip.** Sort roles by the verb. Engineers *build pipelines*, scientists *build models*, machine learning engineers *deploy and monitor* models, and analysts *answer questions and report*.
:::

## Descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive

Analytics is often sorted into four kinds by the question it answers. They are listed here from least to most complex, and each one builds on the one before.

- **Descriptive analytics** answers *what happened?* Sales by month, average delivery time, a dashboard of last week's website visits.
- **Diagnostic analytics** answers *why did it happen?* Drilling into the data to find that sales fell because one product was out of stock.
- **Predictive analytics** answers *what will happen?* A model estimating each customer's chance of canceling next month.
- **Prescriptive analytics** answers *what should we do?* Recommending an action, often by optimizing: how many seats to sell at each price to maximize revenue.

::::example[Classify the question]
Classify each request: (1) "How many users signed up last quarter?" (2) "Which of our users are likely to cancel?" (3) "Why did sign-ups spike on May 3?" (4) "Which discount should we offer each user to keep them?"

:::step
Request 1 asks what happened, so it is descriptive.
:::

:::step
Request 2 asks what will happen, so it is predictive.
:::

:::step
Request 3 asks why something happened, so it is diagnostic.
:::

:::step
Request 4 asks what action to take, so it is prescriptive.
:::

:::answer
Descriptive, predictive, diagnostic, prescriptive.
:::
::::

:::trap
**Common trap.** A question with "will" in it is predictive; a question with "should" is prescriptive. Do not let a recommendation system's predictions distract you if the stem asks which *action* to take.
:::

## Data science and its neighbors [Competitive]

Several nearby fields overlap with data science, and questions often ask how they differ.

- **Statistics** is the mathematical science of learning from data under uncertainty. Data science relies on it and adds computing at scale and application to a domain.
- **Computer science** studies algorithms, software, and systems. Data science borrows its tools.
- **Business intelligence** focuses on reporting and dashboards about past, mostly structured data: descriptive and diagnostic work. Data science goes further, into prediction, experimentation, and unstructured data such as text and images.
- **Machine learning** is a set of methods that let computers learn patterns from data. It is a toolkit inside data science, and also a part of artificial intelligence (Unit 5 covers it).

The thread through all of them: data science is driven by a question, works iteratively, and is judged by whether its answers help someone make a better decision.

:::takeaways
- Data science uses statistics, computing, and domain knowledge to turn data into insight and decisions.
- Conway's Venn diagram: hacking skills, math and statistics knowledge, substantive expertise; all three together is data science. Hacking plus statistics without domain knowledge is machine learning; hacking plus domain knowledge without statistics is the danger zone.
- DIKW from bottom to top: data, information, knowledge, wisdom. Information is data with context.
- Data literacy: reading, working with, analyzing, communicating, and questioning data.
- Roles: analysts report, scientists model, engineers build pipelines, machine learning engineers deploy, DBAs run databases, stewards own data quality and access.
- Analytics: descriptive (what happened), diagnostic (why), predictive (what will happen), prescriptive (what to do), in rising complexity.
- Business intelligence reports on the past; data science adds prediction and experimentation.
:::
