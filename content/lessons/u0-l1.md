Before you learn a single formula, learn the game you are playing. The rules of this event decide how you should study, how you should spend your 50 minutes, and what wins a close finish. This lesson covers the format and scoring and what they mean for your preparation. The full rulebook, with dates and the testing platform, is on the Reference page under The event.

## What the test is

The Data Science & AI event is a single online test: **100 multiple-choice questions in 50 minutes**, taken alone, on your own laptop or tablet, with a proctor in the room. That works out to **30 seconds per question** on average.

The questions come from eight areas, always in the same proportions:

| Area | Questions | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Data Literacy and Foundations | 20 | 20% |
| Probability and Statistics Foundations | 15 | 15% |
| Data Analysis and Statistics for AI | 15 | 15% |
| Tools for Data and AI | 10 | 10% |
| AI Basics | 10 | 10% |
| Machine Learning | 10 | 10% |
| Perception, Representation, and Reasoning | 10 | 10% |
| Privacy and Ethics | 10 | 10% |

You may not bring notes or your own calculator. The testing platform has a built-in calculator, and you get **one sheet of scratch paper**.

:::key
**Key idea.** Half of the test (50 questions) comes from the first three areas: data literacy, statistics, and data analysis. They are also the areas with the most calculation. Strength there is the foundation of a winning score.
:::

## How you are scored, and how ties are broken

Every correct answer is worth **one point**, and a wrong answer costs **nothing**. There is no penalty for guessing, so a blank is simply a lost chance at a free point.

At the top of a national field, many competitors score within a few points of each other, so ties matter. FBLA breaks them in three steps:

1. The number correct on **10 preselected tiebreaker questions**.
2. If still tied, the number correct on **20 preselected questions**.
3. If still tied, the **shortest completion time**.

FBLA does not say which questions are the tiebreakers, but a set chosen to separate the best competitors will lean toward the harder questions. That is why this course goes past the basics into what it calls **Champion** material: the subtle distinctions and multi-step problems that decide the top places.

:::tip
**Test tip.** Speed is the third tiebreaker, not the first. Finish, review the questions you marked, and then submit promptly. Sitting on a finished test for ten minutes can only cost you a tie.
:::

## Rules that affect your score

A few rules can cost points before you answer anything:

- **Five points** are deducted for arriving late or for not following the FBLA Dress Code. That is five questions' worth of work, gone. Arrive early, dressed to code.
- Phones, smartwatches and headphones must be off and stored. A visible device is an Honor Code violation.
- Bring a sharpened pencil, a **fully charged** device, your nametag, and photo ID.
- On the real platform, **flagging a question sends it to FBLA's review team** as a possible error. It is not a bookmark, and it gives you no extra time. If a question truly looks broken, flag it with a short reason ("please review" plus a sentence) and keep moving.

:::trap
**Common trap.** Students assume they can mark questions and see a review screen at the end, as on some school tests. No public source confirms that this platform has one. Keep your own list of question numbers to revisit on your scratch paper.
:::

## What this means for how you prepare

The scoring rules translate into five priorities:

1. **Cover every area.** Each area is worth at least 10 points. A blind spot in a 10-question area costs more than polishing an area you already know.
2. **Train recognition, not just understanding.** Thirty seconds leaves no time to derive a definition. You must know terms well enough to spot the right answer instantly.
3. **Make small calculations automatic.** Means, medians, standard deviations, z-scores, expected values, base conversions and model metrics should take seconds, not minutes.
4. **Practice the hard questions.** The tiebreakers reward accuracy on difficult items.
5. **Practice under real conditions.** The five practice tests in this app use the real length, time limit, mix of areas and a basic calculator.

FBLA describes its objective tests as intentionally rigorous: they aim to reward understanding and application in varied scenarios rather than memorization. So expect questions like "Which method best fits this situation?" as often as "What is the definition of this term?"

## How this course works

Each unit matches one of the eight areas, in the order that builds understanding fastest: data literacy, then statistics, then data analysis, then tools, machine learning, AI, reasoning, and ethics. Every lesson works the same way:

- **Sections** teach one idea at a time, with examples first and the precise definition after. Sections marked **Competitive** go beyond the basics; sections marked **Champion** reach the level that decides national places.
- **Checks** after each section lock in what you just read, with instant feedback and an explanation of every wrong choice.
- **Worked examples** let you try a problem and reveal the solution one step at a time.
- **Key takeaways** at the end are what you reread the night before.
- A **lesson quiz** (8 to 12 questions, timed) tells you whether you have it. The mastery target is 80 percent; below that, reread the sections behind your misses.
- A **unit test** closes each unit with new questions at real test pace.

Outside the lessons, the **Practice** page holds every question type as a skill grid, **Flashcards** keep vocabulary fresh, **Review** collects every mistake, **Progress** shows your projected score, and **Plan** paces everything to your competition date.

:::takeaways
- 100 multiple-choice questions, 50 minutes, eight areas: 20, 15, 15, and five areas of 10.
- One point per correct answer and no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a blank.
- Ties: 10 preselected questions, then 20 more, then completion time. Accuracy on hard questions wins close finishes.
- Five points are lost for lateness or dress-code violations.
- On the real platform, a flag is a challenge sent to FBLA's review team, not a bookmark. Track questions to revisit on scratch paper.
- Prepare for breadth, instant recognition, fast calculation, hard questions, and real test conditions.
:::
