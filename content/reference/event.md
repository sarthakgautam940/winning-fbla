Data Science & AI is an individual objective test for high school members. It was added to FBLA's competitive events for the 2025-2026 season, so 2026-2027 is only its second year. This page collects everything official about how it runs, from the 2026-2027 guidelines (updated August 2026) and FBLA's own operations documents.

## Format

- **100 multiple-choice questions in 50 minutes**, taken online and proctored on site at the National Leadership Conference (NLC).
- That is **30 seconds per question** on average. Most questions should take far less, which buys time for the harder ones.
- No reference or study materials of any kind.
- **No personal calculators.** An online calculator is built into the testing platform. Practice with a basic four-function calculator; that is the safe assumption, and Pennsylvania's version of the guidelines describes a non-graphing calculator with scientific and graphing calculators banned.
- **One sheet of scratch paper**, provided by FBLA and collected after the test. Bring a sharpened pencil.
- Internet access is provided for the testing time only.

## Scoring and tiebreakers

- Each correct answer is worth **one point**. Wrong answers cost nothing, so **never leave a question blank**.
- Ties are broken in three steps:
  1. The number correct on **10 preselected tiebreaker questions**.
  2. If still tied, the number correct on **20 preselected questions**.
  3. If still tied, the **shortest completion time** wins.
- Results announced at NLC are final. At most **10 winners** are recognized per event.
- If a flagged question is removed after review, every score is recalculated.

:::tip
**Test tip.** The tiebreaker questions are chosen in advance, and they are most likely the hardest ones. Accuracy on difficult questions decides close finishes. Speed only matters after both question tiebreakers, so finish your review, then submit; do not sit on a finished test.
:::

## Flagging a question

On the real platform, a **flag is a challenge**, not a bookmark. A flagged question goes to FBLA's Competitive Events Operations Team before results are final, a reviewer recommends an action, and you get **no extra time** for flagging. FBLA recommends typing "please review" and, if time allows, coming back to add a short explanation.

No public source describes a separate "mark for later" list or an end-of-test review screen on the platform. Keep your own list of question numbers to revisit on your scratch paper.

## Penalties and conduct

- **Five points** are deducted for not following the FBLA Dress Code or for arriving late to the testing site. Late arrivals may still test until results are finalized, at staff discretion.
- Phones, smartwatches, other electronic devices and headphones must be **turned off and stored** before the event starts. A visible device is an Honor Code violation.
- Violating the Code of Conduct or Honor Code can lead to disqualification.

## What to bring

- A sharpened pencil.
- A **fully charged** laptop or tablet for online testing (smartphones are not allowed). Chrome is the most recommended browser.
- Your conference-provided nametag.
- Photo identification that matches the name on your badge (driver's license, passport, state ID, or school ID).
- Attire that meets the FBLA Dress Code.

FBLA provides the scratch paper, internet access, and the test login (link and password, given at check-in).

## The testing platform

FBLA's objective tests at NLC run on **Blue Panda E-Testing** at bpet.gobluepanda.com. FBLA's support center points competitors to a **sample test** on the same platform so you can check your device and practice the interface; the event code, username and passcode for the sample are all "sample". Try it on the exact laptop you will bring, well before the conference. Firewalls on school networks can block the site, so test from the network you will use if you can. Virginia's 2026 state conference recommended PCs because of firewall trouble with Macs.

State and regional competitions can use different arrangements. Ask your adviser how your region and state run the test.

## Eligibility for nationals

- National dues must be paid by **11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on March 1** of the school year.
- Each state may send up to **four entries** per event.
- Each member may compete in one individual or team event plus one chapter event at NLC.
- A member who places in the **top 10** of this event at an NLC cannot enter it again at a future NLC.
- You must register for NLC, pay the conference fee, and stay in the official FBLA housing block.

## Who writes the test

MBA Research builds FBLA's objective tests. FBLA describes its objective tests as competitive by design: intentionally rigorous, meant to separate levels of knowledge, and aimed at rewarding understanding and application in varied scenarios rather than memorization. No typical or cut scores are published for any FBLA objective test.

## About this app

Winning FBLA is an independent study tool. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by FBLA, and its practice questions are original. Where state rules differ from national rules, follow your state's.
