# Fact check: FBLA Data Science & AI study app

Verified 2026-11-01. 262 claims in 20 topic tables: 245 VERIFIED, 12 CORRECTED, 5 UNVERIFIED. Full bibliography: `research/sources.md`.

**How to read the tables**

- **Claim** is the sentence the app should teach, worded to stay true over time.
- **VERIFIED**: the usual version is confirmed by the cited source. **CORRECTED**: the usual version (or the version in the brief) is wrong, incomplete or out of date; teach the Claim as written. **UNVERIFIED**: not confirmed from an opened source; do not quiz on it (Notes say what is safe to say instead).
- **Source** lists only pages that were actually opened. Primary sources were preferred. "Wikipedia fallback" in Notes marks uncontroversial details where no primary page could be reached. Several sites blocked automated reading (for example openai.com, reuters.com, nytimes.com, nobelprize.org, acm.org, EUR-Lex, iso.org, hhs.gov); Notes name the substitute used (official reprint, regulator PDF, court record or reputable outlet).
- **Notes** also flag test traps inside VERIFIED rows, such as papers with two valid years (arXiv preprint vs conference).
- The session's web-search quota ran out partway through. Later checks used direct fetches of known URLs, so the few unresolved items stay UNVERIFIED rather than guessed.

**Corrections that change what the app should teach**

1. EU AI Act: the Digital Omnibus on AI was adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 27 July 2026). High-risk rules for Annex III uses now apply from 2 December 2027 (not 2 August 2026), and for AI in Annex I products from 2 August 2028 (not 2 August 2027). A ninth banned practice (non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material) applies from 2 December 2026.
2. OWASP published an LLM Top 10 2026 edition (August 2026) with a different order and a new entry (Hidden Context Exposure). Always pair IDs with their year (LLM01:2025 vs LLM01:2026).
3. SAE J3016 was revised in September 2026 (J3016_202609). The six level names did not change.
4. "Learning without being explicitly programmed" is a later paraphrase, not a quotation from Arthur Samuel's 1959 paper.
5. ImageNet's 2009 paper reported 3.2 million images in 5,247 categories. "14 million images, about 22,000 categories" describes the later full dataset.
6. Apple Card: New York DFS (2021) found no unlawful sex discrimination. The 2024 CFPB penalty concerned dispute handling, not gender bias.
7. Hardt, Price and Srebro (2016) introduced equalized odds and equal opportunity, not demographic parity. Rubin (1976) defined MAR, not the full MCAR/MAR/MNAR scheme. scikit-learn began as a 2007 Google Summer of Code project; the INRIA-led first public release came in 2010.
8. Software defaults differ: pandas `std()` divides by n - 1 but NumPy `np.std()` divides by n. Quartiles differ between the TI-84 ("median of each half"), Tukey hinges, the type 7 default in pandas, NumPy, R and Excel QUARTILE.INC, and Python's `statistics.quantiles` (exclusive by default).

**Time-sensitive rows** (re-check before each competition season): EU AI Act dates; GDPR changes in the data Digital Omnibus (still being negotiated); California ADMT compliance date (1 January 2027); NIST AI RMF revision (1.0 still current); OWASP and C2PA editions; SAE J3016 revision; Clearview AI's UK appeal; ChatGPT user counts; all library and R version numbers.

| k-anonymity, formalized by Latanya Sweeney in 2002 (International Journal on Uncertainty, Fuzziness and Knowledge-Based Systems, vol. 10, no. 5, pp. 557-570), requires that every person's record in a released table be indistinguishable from at least k-1 other records on its quasi-identifiers (attributes such as ZIP code, birth date and gender that can be linked to outside data). | VERIFIED | https://dataprivacylab.org/dataprivacy/projects/kanonymity/kanonymity.pdf ; https://dataprivacylab.org/projects/kanonymity/index.html | Definition 3 in the paper: "each sequence of values in RT[QIRT] appears with at least k occurrences". Journal name appears as "Journal on" in Sweeney's own citation and "Journal of" elsewhere. PDF text read with pdftotext because WebFetch could not parse it. |
| Data are usually made k-anonymous by generalization (replacing a value with a less specific but consistent one, for example ZIP 02138 becomes 0213*) and suppression (not releasing a value at all), as described in Sweeney's companion 2002 paper. | VERIFIED | https://dataprivacylab.org/dataprivacy/projects/kanonymity/kanonymity2.html ; https://dataprivacylab.org/dataprivacy/projects/kanonymity/kanonymity.pdf | Companion paper: "Achieving k-anonymity privacy protection using generalization and suppression", same journal 10(5), 2002, pp. 571-588.
