# Additional verified facts

Facts verified while writing lessons, beyond research/facts.md. Each row was checked against the source on the date shown.

| Claim used in the lessons | Source | Accessed |
|---|---|---|
| AlphaGo's neural networks were "trained by a novel combination of supervised learning from human expert games, and reinforcement learning from games of self-play" (Silver et al., "Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search," Nature 529(7587):484-489, 28 January 2016). | PubMed record of the Nature paper: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26819042/ | 2026-11-02 |
| U.S. Copyright Office (Library of Congress), "Copyright Registration Guidance: Works Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence," Federal Register Vol. 88, No. 51, March 16, 2023: "copyright can protect only material that is the product of human creativity"; "When an AI technology determines the expressive elements of its output, the generated material is not the product of human authorship"; applicants "have a duty to disclose the inclusion of AI-generated content in a work submitted for registration," and AI-generated content that is more than de minimis "should be explicitly excluded from the application." | govinfo copy of the Federal Register notice: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-03-16/html/2023-05321.htm (federalregister.gov redirected to a bot check, not followed) | 2026-11-02 |
| The U.S. Copyright Office's report "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence" has three parts: Part 1, Digital Replicas (July 31, 2024); Part 2, Copyrightability (January 29, 2025), on the copyrightability of outputs created using generative AI; and Part 3, Generative AI Training, released as a pre-publication version on May 9, 2025, with a final version still to come. | https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ | 2026-11-02 |
| The term "foundation models" was introduced in "On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models" (Rishi Bommasani and colleagues, Stanford's Center for Research on Foundation Models, arXiv v1 16 August 2021) for models "trained on broad data at scale" that can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks; the abstract names BERT, DALL-E, and GPT-3 as examples. | arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07258 | 2026-11-02 |
| In 1969 Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert published the book Perceptrons, which "assailed Rosenblatt's work and, essentially, sealed its fate"; enthusiasm for perceptrons waned, and the perceptron's rise and fall "helped usher in" the AI winter. Teach: the book laid out the limits of single-layer perceptrons, and interest in neural networks faded for years. (The MIT Press book page returned 403, so the year and authors rest on this university source.) | Cornell Chronicle, "Professor's perceptron paved the way for AI, 60 years too soon" (title punctuation simplified), Melanie Lefkowitz, 25 September 2019: https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2019/09/professors-perceptron-paved-way-ai-60-years-too-soon | 2026-11-02 |
