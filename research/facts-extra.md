| Harini Suresh and John Guttag, "A Framework for Understanding Sources of Harm throughout the Machine Learning Life Cycle" (EAAMO '21, 2021), "identifies seven distinct potential sources of downstream harm in machine learning": historical bias (harm even when data are perfectly measured and sampled, because the world as it is or was is unfair; example: word embeddings associating "nurse" with women and "engineer" with men); representation bias (the development sample under-represents part of the population; example: about 45% of ImageNet images were taken in the United States, 1% in China and 2.1% in India); measurement bias (features or labels are poor proxies for the target, or are measured differently across groups; example: "arrest" used as a proxy for "crime" in risk assessment); aggregation bias (a one-size-fits-all model for groups that should be treated differently; example: tweets whose emojis and hashtags carry local meanings); learning bias (modeling choices such as the objective amplify disparities; example: pruned or compact models amplifying disparities on underrepresented groups); evaluation bias (benchmark data do not represent the use population, and aggregate metrics hide subgroup underperformance; example: darker-skinned women were only 7.4% and 4.4% of the Adience and IJB-A benchmarks); deployment bias (a model is used differently from the problem it was built to solve; example: risk tools built to predict reoffending used "off-label" to help set sentence length). | https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.10002 (arXiv 1901.10002) | 2026-11-02 |
| Danielle Ensign, Sorelle A. Friedler, Scott Neville, Carlos Scheidegger and Suresh Venkatasubramanian, "Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing" (Proceedings of the 1st Conference on Fairness, Accountability and Transparency, PMLR 81:160-171, 2018): in predictive policing, "Discovered crime data (e.g., arrest counts) are used to help update the model, and the process is repeated"; such systems are "susceptible to runaway feedback loops, where police are repeatedly sent back to the same neighborhoods regardless of the true crime rate"; reported incidents (from residents) "can attenuate the degree of runaway feedback" but "cannot entirely remove it" without the authors' interventions. | https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/ensign18a.html | 2026-11-02 |
