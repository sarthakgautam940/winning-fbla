## 2. The guidelines (task 1.1)

### 2.1 Current edition

- The national guideline is a 5-page PDF with the footer "Data Science & AI - Page x of 5 - Updated August 2026". It is linked as "Event Details & Guidelines" from the fbla.org High School Competitive Events page and is served from FBLA CONNECT (which redirects to FBLA's public file store). [S1, S2] CONFIRMED.
- The High School Competitive Events page says the 2026-2027 guidelines went live at the beginning of September, that the documents may still see small changes, and that members should check the footer date to be sure they have the latest version. [S2] The footer still read "Updated August 2026" on 2026-11-01, so no newer revision exists as of that date. CONFIRMED.
- The National Competitive Events Operations Manual (Education Committee approved December 2, 2025) says guidelines are updated yearly and posted by September 1, and the Education Committee approves recommended changes each December to take effect the following membership year. [S18] INFERRED: the next substantive content change would be for 2027-2028; a mid-season change to this test's objectives is unlikely.
- 2026-2027 High School At-A-Glance (Updated August 2026): Data Science & AI = team size 1, grades 9-12, category Objective Test, internet access provided, 100 questions, 50 minutes, 4 NLC entries per state. [S3] CONFIRMED.
- 2026-2027 Competitive Events Changes (Updated August 2026): the Education Committee kept the existing event structure (only change at structure level: Collegiate Business Case Competition retired). Its list of tests whose knowledge areas and objectives were updated does **not** include Data Science & AI. [S4] A side-by-side check of the 2025-2026 edition (Updated August 2025, hosted by Virginia's CTE Resource Center) against the 2026-2027 edition shows identical knowledge areas, item counts, objectives, and reference list. [S8, S1] CONFIRMED.

### 2.2 Guideline details beyond the app's background facts

All background facts supplied with the task (50 minutes, 100 multiple-choice questions, individual, high school, no reference materials, online calculator in the platform, one sheet of scratch paper, flagging, 1 point per correct answer with no penalty, the three-step tiebreak, maximum 10 winners, 5-point penalty for dress code or lateness, the eight areas and item counts) match the national guideline. [S1] CONFIRMED. Additional points in the same document:

- Internet access is provided for the testing time only. Some events may begin before the Opening Session; NLC schedules are posted in host-city local time; schedule changes are not permitted.
- Competitor brings: a sharpened pencil, a fully powered device for online testing, the conference nametag, photo ID, and dress-code attire. FBLA provides: one piece of scratch paper, internet access, and the test login information (link and password, handed out at test check-in).
- NLC eligibility: national dues paid by 11:59 p.m. Eastern on March 1 of the school year; a member who has placed top 10 in this event at an NLC may not enter it again; must register for NLC and stay in the official FBLA housing block; up to 4 entries per state per event; each member may enter one individual/team event plus one chapter event; valid photo ID matching the badge name.
- Late arrivals may still compete until results are finalized (staff discretion), with a 5-point penalty. Results announced at NLC are final.
- ADA accommodations must be requested through conference registration by the registration deadline. The Operations Manual adds that English Language Learners may bring a physical (not electronic) translation dictionary, and that FBLA may apply time-and-a-half when an ELL plan does not specify an accommodation. [S18]
- Phones, smartwatches, other electronic devices, and headphones must be off and stored; a visible device is an Honor Code violation.
- Educational alignments (shown in the 2025-2026 edition; the 2026-2027 edition shows them only behind a QR code that was not opened): Career Cluster Framework connection = Digital Technology; NACE competencies = Career & Self-Development, Critical Thinking, Technology. [S8]

Two things the Data Science & AI guideline does **not** contain, compared with other 2026-2027 objective-test guidelines such as Advertising [S10]:

1. No "Sample Preparation Resources" bullet (Advertising has one pointing to official sample items in CONNECT).
2. No MBA Research performance-indicator codes or LAP (Learning Activity Package) references next to the objectives (Advertising's objectives carry codes such as "PR:007").

### 2.3 New event, introduced in 2025-2026; replaced nothing

- FBLA's "2025-26 Competitive Events Changes: High School" (Updated July 2025) lists **Data Science & AI (Artificial Intelligence)** as a **New Event: Individual Event, Objective Test**. Its stated rationale is to address the Digital Technology Cluster and the FBLA "pathway" to Data Analysis. [S5] The August 2025 update of the same document (hosted by Virginia's CTE Resource Center) repeats this. [S6] CONFIRMED.
- The national Competitive Events webinar slides (October 7, 2025) list Data Science & AI first under "Overview: New Objective Tests", High School. [S7] CONFIRMED.
- It did not replace anything. Renamed events in that document carry a "Formerly named" line (for example Accounting, Customer Service, Technology Support & Services); Data Science & AI has none. The events retired that year (American Enterprise Project, Partnership with Business Project, Financial Math, Introduction to Financial Math, Public Policy & Advocacy, Spreadsheet Applications, UX Design, Word Processing) each name a different successor; none points to Data Science & AI. [S5] CONFIRMED.
- The "pathway" event is Data Analysis (high school, team of 1-3, presentation). [S3] INFERRED: Data Science & AI is meant as the individual test that leads toward it.
- Consequence for preparation: 2026-2027 is only the event's second season, with one NLC of history (2026), so very little public prep material exists. INFERRED.

### 2.4 Who writes the test, and from what

- MBA Research says it builds FBLA's competitive-event objective tests and is updating FBLA's objective-test study guides for 2025-2026 and 2026-2027. [S11] CONFIRMED. FBLA's 2025-2026 Test Proctor Information template calls FBLA tests proprietary and refers to breaches harming "FBLA or MBA Research". [S17]
- MBA Research hosts an "FBLA HS Data Science and AI" study guide (4 pages) with the same objectives as the guideline. Its footnote says the learning outcomes are based on six sources: Youcubed DS Learning Objectives and Standards, ACM Computing Competencies for Undergraduate Data Science Curricula, CodeHS Intro to AI Syllabus, AI4K12 Grade Band Progression Charts, IBM Data Science Professional Certificate, and Harvard's Data Science Principles. Google Cloud appears only in its reference list, not in the footnote. [S12] CONFIRMED.
- INFERRED: because the Data Science & AI objectives carry no MBA Research performance-indicator codes or LAPs, item writers most likely draw directly from the seven listed references. That is why `research/scope.md` maps those references in detail.
- MBA Research's FBLA page also mentions a test item bank in its Learning Center for teachers (paid). Whether it contains Data Science & AI items: UNVERIFIED. [S11] FBLA's guidelines state that MBA Research materials are not required to prepare. [S10]

### 2.5 State versions of the guideline differ

- Pennsylvania's 2026-2027 edition (7 pages, Updated August 2026) adds region and state sections. At PA region and state conferences a **non-graphing calculator is provided in the online testing program** and scientific or graphing calculators are banned; PA breaks ties by comparing the last 10 questions, then further groups of 10, then submission time. [S9] CONFIRMED (Pennsylvania only). The national tiebreak is different (10 preselected tiebreaker questions, then 20 preselected questions, then completion time). [S1]
- Virginia's rules are in section 6.

## 4. Testing platform, calculator, navigation, flagging (task 1.3)

**Name.** Blue Panda E-Testing. The login page title is "Blue Panda | eTesting", its banner image is labeled "Blue Panda E-Testing", and its address is bpet.gobluepanda.com. The abbreviation "BPET" appears only in the web address; no page spells it out. [S15] FBLA's support article "Online Testing - Devices" (last updated April 8, 2025) points NLC competitors to bpet.gobluepanda.com to try the sample test and tells schools to allow the BPET site through firewalls. [S13] Blue Panda says E-Testing was built for CTSO conferences and that results flow into Blue Panda Register, its competition-management system. State conferences may use other arrangements; Virginia and Pennsylvania both register through Blue Panda [S28, S9], but neither names its objective-testing platform in the documents opened. UNVERIFIED for state level.

Historical note: a 2016-2017 Pennsylvania handout on taking a sample online test links to techfluency.org (Technology Fluency Institute), an older testing service. [S37] Older guides that mention it are out of date.

### 6.2 How Virginia's regional (district) level works

- Virginia uses **regions**, not districts. There are 11: Blue Ridge, Capital, Colonial, Germanna, Longwood, New River, Northern Virginia, Shenandoah, Tidewater, Tri Cities, and UVA at Wise. [S32] Each region has its own bylaws, officers, adviser, and director. [S30] CONFIRMED.
- Each region holds a fall conference and a spring conference; the Regional Director sets dates and sites. [S27] CONFIRMED.
- Data Science & AI at the regional level (Virginia's 2025-2026 guideline): each chapter may enter **two** students; testing is done **at the school site** under a proctor, with careful monitoring; the **top three** in each region are eligible for the SLC. [S8] CONFIRMED for 2025-2026. The 2026 SLC guide repeats that all objective tests advance first, second, and third place from each region. [S28]
- Virginia's 2026-2027 state-specific guideline was not yet posted on the CTE Resource Center on 2026-11-01 (the page still lists 2025-2026 documents). Whether entry or advancement numbers change for 2026-2027: UNVERIFIED. [S31]
- Event limits: at the regional level each student may enter one objective test and one performance event; at the SLC, one objective test, one performance event, one chapter event, and one scholarship. [S33] CONFIRMED (2025-2026).
- SLC objective testing in 2026 (model for 2027): students chose either school-site testing on Thursday April 2 or Thursday April 9, 2026, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., proctored by an adult who is not a business teacher (virtual testers pay the non-attendee registration rate), or on-site testing in Reston (testing center open Friday 11:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. and Saturday 8:30 a.m. to noon, first-come seating, bring your own device). [S28] CONFIRMED.
- Advancement from Virginia's SLC to NLC: the national cap is 4 entries per state per event [S1]; Virginia's rule (for example, top 4) was not found. UNVERIFIED. (Pennsylvania sends its top 4 [S9].)

## 7. Typical or cut scores (task 1.6)

- **No public typical, average, or cut scores** were found for any FBLA objective test, including Data Science & AI. CONFIRMED absence in the sources checked (guidelines, support articles, Operations Manual, state guides).
- FBLA's explanation: objective tests are competitive by design and are not intended for everyone to score perfectly. [S14]
- Scores exist but are private: 2026 NLC scores and rating sheets were released to chapter advisers in Blue Panda (by August 1). [S2]
- If a flagged question is removed, all scores are recalculated. [S18]
- With only one NLC held so far for this event (2026), there is no multi-year history to estimate a winning score. INFERRED.
- Ties at NLC: 10 preselected tiebreaker questions, then 20 preselected questions, then completion time. [S1] Speed only matters after two question-based tiebreaks.

## 8. Implications for the study app (INFERRED)

- Pace: 100 questions in 50 minutes is 30 seconds per question. Timed sets should train that pace.
- Weighting: Data Literacy and Foundations 20 items; Probability and Statistics Foundations 15; Data Analysis and Statistics for AI 15; the other five areas 10 each.
- No penalty for wrong answers, so the habit to train is "answer every question, flag with a note if it looks wrong, move on".
- Calculator: build practice around a basic four-function calculator, plus quick mental math for base conversions, mean/median, expected value, and simple variance.
- One sheet of scratch paper: practice with limited paper.
- State rules can differ from national ones (Virginia school-site regional testing, Pennsylvania's calculator and tiebreak rules); the app should label rules as "national" vs "state".
